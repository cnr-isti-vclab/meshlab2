Texture and UV measures¶

MeshLab reports the statistics of TexMetro, the tool behind the benchmark Real-World Textured Things, as filters, with TexMetro’s own output keys. They run on the CPU, so they give the same numbers on every machine and need no graphics context, and they work on a layer in the document, in the GUI and in scripts.

The filters¶

Filter

What it does

Measure UV Mapping

UV islands, their edges and boundary, folds, mapped fraction, occupancy, and the statistics of the quasi-conformal distortion and of the UV scaling (qcd_*, sf_*), with the UV taken in texels.

Measure Texel Coverage

How the UV triangles cover the texels of each texture, at its size and at every mipmap level (texture_N).

Measure Texture Seam Color Discrepancy

How much the colors of the texture differ across its seams (seam_discrepancy_*).

Measure Topological Properties, Measure Geometric Properties

The mesh keys: counts, boundary, genus, duplicate, zero-area and degenerate faces, orientation, normalized area and boundary length.

Compute UV Distortion

Quasi-conformal distortion and UV scaling per face, as face quality, to look at.

Select Folded UV Faces, Select Overlapping UV Faces

The faces behind nfolds, and the faces on the texels that overwrittenTexels and texelClashes count.

Create Polyline from UV Seams

The seams as a polyline, each edge carrying the discrepancy of its seam as its scalar.

TexMetro’s report from a script¶

docs/examples/texmetro_report.py in the repository runs the filters on a model and writes TexMetro’s JSON file, key for key and in TexMetro’s order, so that scripts written for its output keep working and the two tools can be compared. It needs pymeshlab, or MeshLab’s own Python console, and the model’s texture images next to the model:

python texmetro_report.py model.obj                          # writes texmetro.json
python texmetro_report.py model.obj -t 2048x1024             # a model with no image, as TexMetro's -t
python texmetro_report.py model.obj --compare reference.json # and compare with a file TexMetro wrote

--compare lists the keys that differ by more than a tolerance and exits with 1 if there are any. The tolerances are exact for the counts of the mesh and of the UV layout, a few parts in ten thousand for the common texel counters, a few percent for the rare ones (overwritten, lost, clash), and the six significant digits that TexMetro writes for everything else. They are a starting point, from what is known of where the two differ, and are meant to be set from real comparisons.

Where the numbers differ from TexMetro’s¶

  • Texels are counted on the CPU. TexMetro renders on a GPU, whose rounding of the triangles’ vertices (its subpixel precision: 4 bits on some drivers, 8 on most hardware) moves the rare counters, the overwritten, lost and clash texels, by about 0.1 % to 1 %. MeshLab rounds to 8 bits by default, as most hardware does, and Subpixel Bits changes it. A texel centre exactly on an edge shared by two triangles goes to one of them only.

  • nfolds does not count a face with no UV area as a fold. TexMetro counts it when the sign of its zero differs from its island’s: 20 faces of 1,231 on one real atlas.

  • texelClashes looks at the four diagonal neighbours of a texel, as TexMetro’s code does, whichever was meant; Clash Neighbours offers all eight.

  • seam_discrepancy_* compares, as TexMetro does, the i-th sample of each side’s own edge. The two faces list a shared edge in opposite directions, so this compares a point with the mirrored point of the other side, and reads about 16 % higher than comparing the same point, which Pairing offers.

  • Not reproduced: TexMetro’s recentring of UVs whose box lies outside the unit square, and its zeros for a texture larger than its GPU’s limit (MeshLab counts it, in bands if it is large). orientable is not reported, and oriented is absent for a mesh with non-manifold edges, where an orientation means nothing.

  • Precision. The mesh areas and lengths are summed in single precision, as MeshLab stores the mesh. The distortion and texel measures are in double precision, on a copy of the layer in texels.
