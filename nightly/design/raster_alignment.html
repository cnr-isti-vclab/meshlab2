Raster Alignment¶

How a raster’s camera is aligned to the mesh: by point pairs, by mutual information, both at once, and globally over a set of rasters. How rasters, cameras and lenses are stored is in Data Model; the interactive tool and picking in the raster view are in Rendering. The record of how this was built, with the measurements and the decisions behind it, is history/raster_alignment.md.

What changes and what does not¶

Every alignment changes where a raster’s camera stands and looks, and optionally its focal length, and nothing else. The principal point, the pixel size, the viewport and the lens are the calibration’s (from a project, COLMAP or a photogrammetry tool). Old MeshLab reset the principal point to the image centre on every alignment; nothing here does.

All of it works on the pinhole camera. A raster’s photo is undistorted when its plane is decoded (Document::ensureRasterPlaneImage), so a pixel of the image MeshLab shows is a pixel of RasterEntry::shot. Only perspective cameras are aligned; an orthographic one is refused.

Pixels in point pairs are in the camera’s viewport, from the top-left corner, y down, with pixel centres at k + 0.5. That is also the image’s size whenever the image matches its camera, the usual case. vcg::Shot::Project counts y up, so the filters convert: v_vcg = height − v. The JSON is [{"point": [x, y, z], "pixel": [u, v]}, ...], with points in world space, read by parseCorrespondences (plugins/filter_camera/mutualinformation.h).

The filters¶

All four are in plugins/filter_camera, category Document/Camera.

Filter

What it does

Set Raster Camera (fromView)

Gives the current raster the camera of a view (a camerastate parameter, the current 3D view by default), for the raster’s own image size, roll and orthographic projection included: a starting camera for the others, old MeshLab’s Get Shot. Without fromView, a camera from a viewpoint, a direction and a focal length.

Align Current Raster to Mesh by Point Correspondences

The camera that minimizes the RMS reprojection error of three or more pairs, four with the focal length (vcg::ShotResection::Fit). Returns rmsBefore, rmsAfter, maxAfter, pairErrors.

Align Current Raster to Mesh by Mutual Information

Refines the camera by maximizing the mutual information between the photo and a rendering of the visible layers (Corsini et al. 2009); optionally with point pairs as a soft constraint. Returns informationBefore, informationAfter, evaluations, displacement, and with pairs pairsBefore, pairsAfter.

Align Visible Rasters Globally by Mutual Information

Refines the visible rasters together, each against the mesh painted with its neighbours’ photos (Dellepiane and Scopigno 2013); hidden rasters are anchors. Returns iterations, variations, arcs, evaluations.

The Raster Alignment Tool drives the first two from the raster view: pairs are made by dragging from the mesh to the photo, Fit runs the correspondence filter, and Mutual Information runs the mutual-information filter with the tool’s pairs.

The parametrization¶

vcg::ShotResection<S>::Parametrization (vcglib/vcg/math/shot_resection.h) describes a change of a camera for a derivative-free optimizer. The parameters are:

  • a rotation vector and a translation, in the camera’s own frame, about a pivot;

  • a change of FocalMm, when the focal length is fitted.

Each is scaled so that a unit step moves the projections of the sample points by about one pixel, root mean square. One trust radius in pixels therefore means the same for all of them. The scales are measured on the samples, so the result is deterministic.

The correspondence fit pivots on the centroid of its points; mutual information pivots on the centre of the visible layers’ box, as old MeshLab did. Both minimize with NEWUOA (vcglib/wrap/newuoa) in rounds, each starting where the last ended with the scales measured again, until a round gains nothing.

The rendering¶

Mutual information compares the photo with a rendering made on the CPU, so it is deterministic and runs in ctest and pymeshlab2 without a GPU:

  • The rasterizer. rasterizeTriangleDepth (src/core/softdepthbuffer.h) calls back each pixel a triangle wins the depth test. The renderer shades there, with perspective-correct barycentrics, as the GPU interpolates.

  • What is rendered. Every visible layer with faces, at a working resolution whose longest side is resolution (800). The photo is scaled to the same size and turned to grey as 0.3 R + 0.59 G + 0.11 B.

  • Renderings. Each value is the one channel old MeshLab’s OpenGL programs read back:

    • Combined (the default): the vertex color blended into the normal map by t = red². Where the vertex color is white, as on a mesh without color, it is the normal map.

    • Normal Map: the eye-space normal’s y, mapped to 0 to 1.

    • Vertex Color: its red.

    • Silhouette: the layers white on black.

  • Mutual information is computed over a 128 × 128 joint histogram, in bits:

    • the rendering’s darkest bin, which holds the background, counts 1 / backgroundWeight (default 2);

    • the photo’s transparent pixels, such as an undistorted photo’s corners, do not count.

  • Cost. About 3.8 ms a rendering on 20k faces at 750 × 502, and 50 ms on 3.2 M faces at 800 px. A refinement takes 100 to 300 renderings.

The rendering decides whether the method works on a given photo. On a real colored scan the Normal Map has a higher maximum 13 to 19 px away from the right camera, while Vertex Color finds it within 1.3 px.

Mutual information with point pairs¶

With point pairs, the single-raster filter minimizes old MeshLab’s combined score:

(1 − w)(2 − MI) + w · d

  • d is the pairs’ mean distance in working pixels;

  • w is pairsWeight (default 0.01);

  • w = 0 is mutual information alone, and w = 1 the pairs alone.

The sum is of bits and pixels, so w is a matter of balance rather than of units. Near the optimum, mutual information changes by hundredths of a bit while a pair moves by pixels, so useful weights are small. At 0.01, a few pairs keep the camera in the right basin and leave the last pixel to the information. Heavier weights hand the result to the pairs.

Global refinement¶

Align Visible Rasters Globally by Mutual Information follows the paper:

  • Arcs. The arc from raster j into raster i weighs w = MI(I_i, proj(I_j, I_i)) × OV, where OV is the share of the layers seen from i that j’s photo paints. An arc is made only where OV > 0.2.

  • Painting. A painting rendering projects each photo onto the surface, sampled bilinearly where the photo’s own camera sees it (its depth buffer, within 1%). The photos are combined by their weights in grey levels, and the chosen rendering is used where none sees the surface.

  • A pass. It refines one raster at a time: the one with the most arcs from refined rasters, ties going to the one with more arcs. The raster is refined against the mesh painted with the photos of all its arcs, weighted by them, and then the arcs that involve it are weighed again. A raster with no arcs is skipped.

  • Stopping. Passes repeat until one moves the projections of 5,000 samples by less than threshold px on average (1.2), in each raster’s own pixels, or after maxIterations (5).

  • Anchors. Hidden rasters with a camera count as refined from the start. They paint the mesh and never move.

  • Focal length. It is refined by default, as in the paper’s seven parameters.

Tests¶

In tests/test_filters.cpp, behavioural tier:

  • Set Raster Camera from a view: every corner of a cube lands within 0.01 px of where the view draws it, perspective and orthographic, with a roll.

  • Point correspondences:

    • On the gargoyle fixture’s cameras, from exact pairs: back to under 1e-4 px, also with the focal length.

    • On the 2930Colmap dataset, when present, from COLMAP’s observations taken through the lens: 0.05 px from COLMAP’s camera.

  • Mutual information:

    • Gargoyle cameras from 2 and 5 degree starts: back to where a fit from the true camera ends, within 2 px (5 for camera 1, whose maximum is the flattest).

    • With six noisy pairs, camera 1 from 8 degrees, beyond the information’s own reach: within 1.5 px. The Silhouette rendering runs.

    • On the scan, when present, with Vertex Color: within 3 px of COLMAP’s camera.

  • Global refinement: the gargoyle’s four cameras end within 1.5 px of the fixture’s, and within 2 px with camera 0 as an anchor that does not move.

The tool, which needs the GUI, was checked in the real window, on Metal and OpenGL (see the record).
