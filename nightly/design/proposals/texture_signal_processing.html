Texture Signal Processing Filters¶

This document proposes which of the programs in Prada and Kazhdan’s TextureSignalProcessing (TSP) are worth turning into MeshLab filters, and how to integrate them. It recommends doing so in process, as Screened Poisson is, through TSP’s header-only GradientDomain library, and not as helper programs, as TextureConsolidation is. Stages 1 and 2 implemented 2026-11-12 (the plugin filter_texture_signal_processing, with Repair Texture Seams, Dilate Texture across Seams, Smooth Texture and Sharpen Texture; see its UPSTREAM.md); stage 3 is not. Every number below was measured for this proposal.

See also: TextureConsolidation history, plugins/filter_texture_consolidation/UPSTREAM.md, plugins/filter_screened_poisson/UPSTREAM.md, Adding a Filter, Vocabulary, Filter Organization, TexMetro.

What TSP is¶

TSP solves gradient-domain problems on the texels of a texture atlas. It builds mass and stiffness matrices over the active texels and couples texels across chart seams through the surface, so a filtered signal is continuous on the surface even where it jumps in the image. This is the method of Prada, Kazhdan, Chuang and Hoppe, SIGGRAPH 2018. It is MIT-licensed and builds on Kazhdan’s Misha utility library (BSD-3-Clause). MeshLab already builds both, as submodules of external/texture-consolidation, at upstream commits that include our fixes (mkazhdan/TextureSignalProcessing#19, mkazhdan/Misha#8).

The repository has nine programs, plus a header-only library, include/Src/GradientDomain.h. The library’s constructor takes functors: corner to surface vertex, vertex to position, corner to texture vertex, texture vertex to UV, and the image size. In return it gives the active texels, the texel graph’s edges and whether an edge crosses a chart, and the mass, stiffness and divergence operators, both as Eigen matrices and as direct operators on a texel array. HierarchicalGradientDomain adds a multigrid solver.

Evidence¶

All on 2026-11-12, on this Mac (16 cores: 12 performance, 4 efficiency), Release builds, Apple clang, OpenMP from Homebrew’s libomp. The probes and builds were kept in the session scratchpad, not committed. The test mesh is tests/sample_mesh/gargoyle_small/gargoyle.ply (20k faces) parametrized with Parametrize by Atlas (xatlas). The real texture is 1024², made from the fixture’s four photos by Transfer Color from Visible Rasters to Texture.

  • Eigen. MeshLab compiles against vcglib/eigenlib, Eigen 5.0.1, and TSP vendors Eigen 3.3.7. Both library examples compile against Eigen 5.0.1, and their output images are byte-identical to the builds with TSP’s own Eigen. One Eigen in the binary is therefore possible, and it has to be one: two versions of Eigen’s templates in one process would break C++’s one-definition rule.

  • Misha. TSP carries its own copy of Misha under third_party/include/Misha, and it does not compile with Apple clang (Polynomial.h: “declaration of ‘Degree’ shadows template parameter”). The Misha repository does compile. TSP therefore needs both repositories, as TextureConsolidation already arranges.

  • Names. Misha’s code is in namespace MishaK, and no plugin in the application uses it today. Screened Poisson’s vendored copy is in namespace PoissonRecon. The two can link into one binary.

  • What an adapter would include. A file including only Src/PreProcessing.h, Src/GradientDomain.h and Misha/Texels.h pulls in 28 TSP headers (11,874 lines) and 41 Misha headers (22,121 lines), nothing from third_party/. Among them is Misha’s PlyFile.h, which carries the Stanford PLY notice. Its no-sale clause is already an open item in UPSTREAM.md, and in process it would be in the application itself, not only in a helper.

Memory is the main cost, and it is all in setup. In SetExteriorTexels, building the GradientDomain takes the time and the memory. Factoring and solving take under 0.05 s. Memory grows with the image’s side, not its area (2.1, 4.5, 9.1 GB for 512², 1024², 2048² on the same 20k-face mesh). Stage 0 found where it goes: about three quarters is the divergence operator, which these filters do not use.

Seams. Two checks:

  • Reaction-diffusion. Sampling the 512² stripe texture on both sides of all 3,515 seam edges gives a mean difference of 0.004, against 0.075 for one texel’s step inside a chart. Rendered on the mesh, no seam shows.

  • Real texture. Measure Texture Seam Color Discrepancy on the photo texture drops from a mean of 26.4 (max 164) to 20.5 (max 120) after SetExteriorTexels. What remains is real color disagreement between the photos on the two sides of a seam, which this program does not touch by design. It only makes bilinear lookups agree.

Two preconditions of the library¶

  1. No chart may come near the image border. On the xatlas atlas, whose charts come within 0.4 texel of the edge, the library throws “Could not factor node index” (BoundaryQuadraticElements.inl, GetBoundaryPolygons). With the UVs pulled 4 texels in from the edge, it runs. The programs avoid this by enlarging the grid with Src/Padding.h and cropping afterwards. The adapter has to do the same, rather than change the user’s UVs.

  2. Some errors call exit(). Misha’s MK_ERROR_OUT prints a message and calls exit(0). Three library headers use it: SimpleTriangleMesh.inl, HierarchyConstruction.inl and MultiIndex.h. In process, that would close MeshLab. The fix is the one-line redefinition TextureConsolidation’s wrapper already uses, #define MK_ERROR_OUT( ... ) MK_THROW( __VA_ARGS__ ) placed before anything includes Misha. It turns the exit into a MishaK::Exception for the filter to catch.

Padding is a separate, user-facing condition¶

xatlas’s default padding is 0, so at 512² neighbouring charts can share texels. With the default, 148 of the 3,515 seam edges disagree by more than 0.1; with padding 2, 2 do. Any texel-space filter should refuse such an atlas, or warn about it. Select Overlapping UV Faces already finds the faces involved.

Stage 0: memory and robustness¶

Measured 2026-11-12. The method: an instrumented copy of TSP’s include/Src, kept in the scratchpad and placed ahead of the submodule’s on the include path. It prints the process’s current and peak physical footprint (task_info, the figure /usr/bin/time -l reports) between the steps of GradientDomain’s construction. AddressSanitizer builds were used for the crashes.

Where the memory goes¶

All of it is in OperatorInitializer::Initialize: the hierarchy before it peaks at 0.9 GB of the 9.1 GB at 2048². Within it, the largest single step is InitializeBoundaryCoarseToFineBoundaryOneFormProlongation (EdgeIndexing.h). At 2048² it builds 58 million triplets over 4.5 million fine boundary edges and adds 3.6 GB. Building the divergence matrix after it adds another 1.4 GB. Both, and the edge indexing before them, exist only for the divergence operator. GradientDomain always builds that operator, but SetExteriorTexels, TextureDilation and uniform smoothing never use it. OperatorInitializer already has an overload without it, used by LineIntegralConvolution.

The memory follows the number of fine boundary edges: the edges of the quadratic elements made by clipping mesh triangles to the texels a chart boundary crosses. These are 4.5 million for the 20k-face mesh at 2048², and 32.7 million for the 3M-face mesh at 4096². That count is why memory tracks the image’s side rather than its area. It also means memory tracks how much chart boundary the atlas has, so a fragmented photogrammetry atlas costs far more than xatlas’s 300 charts.

Two smaller savings were tried and are not worth proposing. In _Initialize and the one-form merge, per-chart and per-thread buffers are copied into one while the originals stay alive. Releasing each one as it is copied keeps the order and the output, but lowers the peak by only about 5% (9.20 → 8.75 GB, 2.61 → 2.45 GB at 2048²; 15.8 → 12.4 GB at 4096² without divergence, with the robustness fixes below).

Conclusion: the memory is inherent to TSP’s boundary representation, not a leak. It can be cut by about 3.5× by not building an operator these filters do not use, an upstream change: a constructor flag on GradientDomain, with the current behaviour as the default. With it, a 4096² texture on a 3M-face scan needs about 12–16 GB, which a desktop has but a filter running inside MeshLab should not take silently. Stage 1 therefore keeps the in-process plan, and the filter reports its estimate before it runs (see open question 1).

TSP does not run on the real 3M-face atlas¶

tests/local/COLOR_gargoyle/gargo3M_good.ply with its 4096² texture crashes TSP before memory becomes a question. That happens in the unmodified programs too, not only in the instrumented copy. Under AddressSanitizer, one fault at a time:

  1. A chart-vertex indexing bug (AtlasCharts.inl, getCharts). The map from atlas vertex to chart vertex is one array shared by all charts. A UV vertex used by two charts that touch only at that vertex gets, in the second chart, an index into the first chart’s array. On this atlas, 167 vertices (249 corners) are shared that way. 129 of the corners read past the array, which is the crash (heap corruption, reported by the allocator in InitializeGridChartsActiveNodes). The other 120 silently take a vertex from the wrong chart. The xatlas atlas has none. This is a bug independent of the input’s validity. The fix keeps a per-chart owner, with a map for the rare other charts. It is verified byte-identical on the xatlas atlas.

  2. Empty clipped polygons. One clipping function reads index −1 of an emptied polygon: ClipIndexedIntersectionPolygonToIndexedIntersectionEdge, the sibling of the function fixed in mkazhdan/TextureSignalProcessing#19. Or, when the last of the triangle’s edges empties it, GetTriangulation is handed a polygon with no vertices and reports that through MK_ERROR_OUT: the program exits with status 0, the failure mode the error hook above is for.

    Two inputs empty a polygon:

    • The 30,085 zero-area UV triangles (1%). They have no inside. With them still in, the run faults later, in IndexConverter::combinedToBoundary. Not fixed.

    • A valid triangle that only touches a boundary cell. Upstream’s rasterizer reports every cell the closed triangle meets, but the clip keeps only what is strictly inside. So an edge through a texel centre, which float UVs can hit exactly, leaves nothing. Even with the zero-area triangles removed, exactly one triangle on this atlas does this, so stage 0’s runs completed only because they carried the guards. A square split along its diagonal reproduces it at 64². This is upstream’s bug whatever the input, and a pull request skips the empty cell at both call sites, as TSP’s quadrature code already does.

  3. Flipped UV triangles (1,211 on this atlas). These fault in _InitializeChart (Operators.inl:1132). TSP’s README requires a valid mapping, and the programs’ --sanityCheck exists to test for one.

The patches for 1 and 2 are kept in .reference/texture-consolidation-patches/tsp-stage0/. They are not upstream. The 4096² figures above are on this atlas with its zero-area and flipped faces removed (2,968,618 faces left), using the fix for 1.

What this means for the filters:

  • Fix 1 and the touching-triangle half of fix 2 belong upstream, and both pull requests were prepared on 2026-11-12 (files in .reference/texture-consolidation-patches/tsp-chart-vertex/ and tsp-clip-graze/). Fix 1 is #20. An earlier PR, #17, fixes the same bug by overwriting the vertex’s owner. On this atlas that adds 89 vertices a second time to a chart that already had them, and the texture differs from #20’s at 228 texels, by up to 54/255.

  • For the other two, the adapter should keep zero-area UV triangles out of what it hands TSP. They cover no texels, so leaving them out changes nothing in texel space. Upstream may prefer a fix, which can follow.

  • Flipped triangles are a different matter. They do cover texels. Left out, those texels would count as outside every chart, and Repair Texture Seams would overwrite them. The filter should refuse an atlas with flipped or overlapping faces and point to Select Folded UV Faces and Select Overlapping UV Faces, as the padding rule does for shared texels.

Integration: in process, as Screened Poisson is¶

TextureConsolidation runs as helper programs because its programs are whole command-line pipelines (back-projection, optical flow, stitching) that read and write files. TSP’s library is a different case. It is built for being called, and it is called with functors that a CMeshO with wedge UVs answers directly.

Recommendation: in process, in a new plugin filter_texture_signal_processing (Texture Signal Processing Filters: one dependency, one plugin, as in Filter Organization). The adapter does three things: it pads the grid, it wraps errors, and it converts between CMeshO/QImage and TSP’s functors and arrays. The memory finding was the one thing that could reverse this choice. Stage 0 showed it is inherent, and about 3.5× smaller without the divergence operator. A helper process would not reduce it; it would only keep it out of MeshLab’s address space. So the choice stands.

The Stanford PLY notice (open question 3), decided the same day. In process, it was a licence conflict, not a packaging question. SimpleTriangleMesh.h included Misha’s PLY reader, and Ply.inl’s namespace-scope PLY::EdgeProps array was emitted into any object including GradientDomain.h, even at -O2. That would have linked “do not sell” code into GPLv3 MeshLab. #23 moves the three mesh I/O members into MeshIO.h, which only the programs include. After it, an adapter-like file includes no PLY header and emits no PLY symbol.

Staging¶

  1. Memory. Done 2026-11-12; see Stage 0. Left from it are three upstream pull requests: the chart-vertex fix (#20), the touching-triangle clip (#21), and a GradientDomain flag, computeDivergence, to skip the divergence operator. With the flag, SetExteriorTexels peaks at 15.6 GB instead of 44.5 GB on the 3M-face atlas at 4096², with byte-identical output.

  2. Plugin and adapter, two filters. Implemented 2026-11-12, with these measured differences from the plan: TSP is its own submodule (above); the adapter pads only atlases that come within two texels of the border, since a margin changes the result where texel centers lie on an edge; it never writes a texel another chart covers, which SetExteriorTexels can; it fuses texture coordinates as TSP’s reader does; and it pulls each exterior texel towards its current value with a weight of 1e-2 of the median coupling (decided 2026-11-12). Without it, the exterior texels a triangle only grazes are barely determined and come out far out of range, white and black dots along the chart boundaries, as SetExteriorTexels gives them; with it the dots drop from 22,220 to 2,628 on the 3M-face scan, the seam discrepancy on the gargoyle test is slightly lower (28.03 against 28.26), and a per-triangle layout no longer makes the system singular. Overlapping atlases are not refused, since they do not fault. In the application, on the 3M-face scan at 4096², the repair takes 20 s and 17 GB, the dilation 7 s and 5 GB. The adapter keeps zero-area UV triangles out of TSP’s input, and refuses atlases with flipped or overlapping faces. Repair Texture Seams (SetExteriorTexels) and Dilate Texture across Seams (TextureDilation). Decided 2026-11-12:

    • both names, the subcategory Texture/Filtering, and widening Dilate to images;

    • memory is documented in the descriptions, and the filters just run (open question 1);

    • the dilation samples the input image and writes a separate output. TextureDilation’s own main samples the image it is writing, which makes its output differ from run to run when threaded. Sampling a copy makes it repeatable.

    Tests:

    • a smoke row for each;

    • a behavioural test asserting that Measure Texture Seam Color Discrepancy drops on the gargoyle photo texture;

    • the xatlas gargoyle at its default settings, whose charts touch the image border: the adapter pads, so the filter must run rather than throw.

  3. Smooth Texture, Sharpen Texture, with an optional face selection to localize them. Implemented 2026-11-12. Decided the same day: the two parameters are TSP’s own (Fidelity, its interpolation weight, default 1000; Gradient Scale, its modulation, 0 to smooth and 2 to sharpen); the selection builds the divergence operator only when used; the solver is multigrid until converged (open question 5: the direct solver took 63 s and 5.7 GB to factor at 2048²). On the gargoyle test, smoothing takes the seam discrepancy from 33.9 to 2.9. On the 3M-face scan at 4096²: 3 minutes and 19 GB, 4 minutes and 47 GB with a selection. Found on the way: TSP jittered with rand(), so in the application every run differed from the last; #24 gives the jitter a generator of its own.

  4. Line-integral convolution, reaction-diffusion, source-aware stitching. Each needs its own decision first: the vocabulary for the first two, Paolo’s ruling on the reaction term, and a change to color projection for the third.

Naming¶

Vocabulary has no subcategory for processing an existing image. Texture has Assignment, Conversion and Packing. Proposed:

  • A subcategory Texture/Filtering, for filters that change a texture’s texels from its own texels. All stage-1 and stage-2 filters go there.

  • Repair Texture Seams: Repair (“fix a defect while preserving intent”) fits as defined.

  • Smooth Texture, Sharpen Texture: Sharpen is admitted for attributes; Smooth is defined for positions and normals but already used for colors (Smooth Vertex Color). The vocabulary should say so.

  • Dilate Texture across Seams: Dilate is admitted only for selections. Widening it to images keeps its standard morphological meaning. The alternative, Fill, is rejected in the lexicon as a synonym of Close.

  • Line-integral convolution and reaction-diffusion produce a new texture from no input texture. Create is reserved for layers, and Render means an image of the scene. To be decided with the stage-3 filters.

Open questions¶

  1. Memory in the filter. Stage 0 explained the memory: it follows the fine boundary edge count. The filter should still say what it is about to take. Two options: estimate it from a cheap pass over the boundary cells and ask before running, or document the figures and run. Which one is the user’s call.

  2. The remaining zero-area fault (combinedToBoundary), for an upstream fix rather than the adapter’s workaround, if mkazhdan prefers one.

  3. The Stanford PLY notice. Moving it from the helpers into the application makes the existing open item in UPSTREAM.md more pressing. Alternatively, the adapter could avoid PlyFile.h, if TSP’s headers can be included without it. That has not been tried.

  4. Reaction-diffusion’s reaction term (stage 3): keep the quadrature (ScalarIntegrator, lower-level API) or approximate it.

  5. Multigrid or direct. Answered with stage 2: at 512² the direct solve is faster than enough V-cycles to match it (2.5 s against 7.5 s for 100), but at 2048² it takes 63 s and 5.7 GB to factor against 28 s of multigrid, and grows faster from there. Multigrid.
