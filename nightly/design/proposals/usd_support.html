USD Support¶

This document proposes reading and writing Pixar’s Universal Scene Description (.usda, .usdc, .usdz) in MeshLab, with its materials, and exporting a set of layers to one USD file. Stage 1, the flattened import and the one-layer export, is implemented (plugins/io_usd); stages 2 and 3 are not. Decided 2026-11-23 (Paolo): the library is LightUSD, and import starts flattened, as glTF’s does.

See also: Data Model (layers, materials, units), Project persistence (how saving works, and why a save is not a filter), Third-Party Code, Rendering (the material channels the renderer reads), plugins/io_gltf (the precedent for materials and for flattening a scene).

1. What USD asks of MeshLab¶

A USD file is a scene, not a mesh: a tree of prims with transforms, many meshes, instances, materials bound per face subset, cameras, and composition arcs (references, payloads, variants, sublayers) that assemble a stage from several files. Five properties matter for us:

  • Geometry is UsdGeomMesh with faceVertexCounts and faceVertexIndices: polygons of any size, not triangles. Attributes are primvars with an interpolation each: constant, uniform (per face), vertex, varying and faceVarying (per corner). Texture coordinates are usually primvars:st, faceVarying, with v up as in MeshLab and OBJ. A mesh can be leftHanded, which reverses the winding.

  • Materials are bound to a whole mesh, or to the faces of a UsdGeomSubset (family materialBind). The portable surface is UsdPreviewSurface, fed by UsdUVTexture nodes, each of which reads one or more channels of an image, with a scale and a bias.

  • Units and orientation are stage metadata: metersPerUnit, whose fallback by the specification is 0.01 (centimeters), and upAxis, whose fallback is Y. MeshLab is Z-up.

  • Packaging: .usdz is an uncompressed zip with the stage and its textures inside, the form Apple’s Quick Look, Blender, Omniverse and Houdini exchange.

  • Composition: a file can be a root layer that references others; what the user sees is the composed stage.

OpenUSD’s packaging is at odds with a statically linked application; LightUSD is the kind of dependency MeshLab already takes. Its risk is youth: a rename and a 1.0 weeks old. Its schema table marks UsdGeom (Mesh and GeomSubset included), UsdPreviewSurface, UsdUVTexture, UsdTransform2d and the primvar readers done.

3. Stage 0: measuring LightUSD¶

  • The submodule at a release tag, built into a throwaway target; build time and the size it adds to the application.

  • Read: the fixtures of section 7, and a few public USDZ assets (Apple’s AR Quick Look gallery, Pixar’s Kitchen Set), through Tydra’s render scene; check subsets, faceVarying texture coordinates, leftHanded, instancing and a referenced file.

  • Write a mesh with two materials and faceVarying coordinates as .usda, .usdc and .usdz; usdchecker and usdcat must accept all three, and .usdz must open in Quick Look.

  • Outcome: go, or go with patches upstreamed first, or reconsider.

3.1 Results (2026-11-27)¶

Measured on LightUSD v1.0.0 (source tarball, not yet a submodule), built as a subdirectory of a throwaway CMake project with Release flags on this 16-core Mac, checked with OpenUSD 26.11 (usd-core from PyPI). The trial program and its outputs stayed outside the repository; the written .usda, .usdz and texture are in the git-ignored tests/local/usd_stage0/.

The library to use is not the one this proposal named. LightUSD 1.0 ships two implementations. The default, src/next (lightusd::next, with the render conversion tydra_next), is a rewrite. The older core, with the Tydra described in its README and lightusd_static in its integration notes, is deprecated (LIGHTUSD_NATIVE_PRODUCT=legacy). Stage 1 targets the new one: link lightusd::next and tydra_next, include next/lightusd-next.hh and tydra/next/render-converter.hh.

Authoring is low-level. The writer builds prims with StageBuilder/LayerBuilder, values with Value::MakeFloatCompArray(..., TypeId::Point3f, 3) and the like, and records the USD type name of each property itself. A shader output (outputs:surface) is declared with add_property_slot on an interned name: a type name alone writes nothing, and the first attempt produced connections to undeclared outputs. The materialBind family must declare its type (subsetFamily:materialBind:familyType = "partition"): OpenUSD’s validator flagged its absence. The exporter is therefore some 300 lines of careful code, not a call. Both pitfalls belong in its comments and its round-trip test.

Verdict: go. Everything stage 1 needs works, at a cost in build time and size MeshLab already pays for comparable libraries.

4. Stage 1: the io_usd plugin¶

A MeshIOPlugin, as io_gltf is, for usda, usdc, usd and usdz.

4.1 Import, flattened¶

4.2 Materials, stage 1¶

Only what MeshIOMaterialSlot can hold today, the four channels glTF fills:

UsdPreviewSurface input

MeshLab slot field

diffuseColor from a UsdUVTexture

baseColorTexture

diffuseColor constant

vertex color, as glTF does with its base color factor

normal from a texture (sourceColorSpace raw, scale 2, bias −1)

normalTexture; normalScale 1

occlusion

occlusionTexture; occlusionStrength 1

roughness, texture or constant

roughnessTexture / roughnessFactor

metallic, emissiveColor, opacity, opacityThreshold, clearcoat, ior, the specular workflow

dropped, with a log line naming them

other surfaces (MaterialX, renderer-specific shaders)

not interpreted; the mesh keeps displayColor

Textures come from files beside the stage or from inside the .usdz; the latter load as in-memory assets with no file, as a .glb’s do, and Save Mesh to .usdz keeps them inside. UDIM paths (<UDIM>) are not supported in stage 1, and say so.

A known limit that stage 1 inherits. UsdUVTexture reads named channels: roughness is often the green channel of a packed image whose blue is metallic, as in glTF. The renderer reads the roughness texture as the mean of its three channels (fill_smooth.frag, roughSample), so a packed texture already renders wrong roughness from glTF today. Stage 3 adds the channel; stage 1 records the image and logs the channel it ignored.

.usda is text, .usdc binary, .usdz packs the textures. Textures follow the Save Mesh rule: stored and unchanged ones are referred to or copied byte for byte, new ones written next to the file, or inside it for .usdz. Unlike glTF’s saver, which writes only the base color texture, all four channels are written.

4.4 As implemented (2026-11-27)¶

plugins/io_usd/usdplugin.cpp, about 1,150 lines (glTF’s is 2,100), builds LightUSD as a subproject with its tools, tests, C API, bundled importers, audio and EXR off. The plugin compiles with QT_NO_KEYWORDS, because LightUSD names members slots.

Departures from 4.1–4.3, each forced by what MeshLab or LightUSD offers:

Implemented

VCGFace has no per-corner normal (src/core/vcgmesh.h); the OBJ path’s IOM_WEDGNORMAL is a mask bit with no storage behind it in MeshLab’s mesh

Also: a prim whose world transform mirrors (negative determinant) has its winding reversed, as leftHanded does; invisible prims are skipped; UsdGeomPoints become vertices; textures inside a .usdz are decoded with QImage from the package (LightUSD’s USDZReader), into in-memory assets; on export a mesh with one slot on every face binds its material on the mesh, otherwise through GeomSubsets whose family is a partition, or nonOverlapping when some faces have no slot; edges are not written, with a log line; texture paths not copied are written relative to the file, as PLY’s and OBJ’s are.

A zero save mask writes everything the format holds, as glTF’s saver does: white vertex colors, a zero scalar primvar and normals included, and the vertex colors displace face colors. The GUI never passes one (defaultSaveMaskForMesh in mainwindow.cpp starts from the layer’s ioMask), but Document::saveMesh callers without a mask do: Python’s save_current_mesh and project saves. Moving that default into Document::saveMesh would fix it for every format; it is outside this stage.

Measured on build-release, this Mac:

  • tests/test_document.cpp: four USD tests (the fixtures in tests/data/usd/, and a usda/usdc/usdz round trip of polygons, wedge coordinates, face colors, a material, an in-memory texture and the unit) and the decimal-comma locale test extended to usda.

  • OpenUSD 26.11 (usd-core, outside the tree) on 12 files MeshLab wrote (the fixtures, a 1,280-face sphere with face colors and the stage-0 cube, each as usda, usdc and usdz): all read as written, all 30 validators report nothing, the .usdz textures resolve inside the package. Not automated: usd-core is not a test dependency.

  • All 1,272 .usd* files in LightUSD’s repository opened one by one through --script: no crash, no hang; 508 load, 764 are refused with a message (no visible mesh or points, or invalid on purpose). The two refused models/ files with a Mesh have no points, or reference an .obj, which needs LightUSD’s OBJ plugin.

  • 4.5 M triangles: load 0.27 s, save 0.66 s, peak resident memory 2.2 GB.

  • Not checked: Quick Look and Blender on a written .usdz; the GUI’s open and save dialogs.

5. Stage 2: scenes¶

5.1 The interface¶

A plugin returns one mesh. A scene needs the layers, each with its matrix, label, visibility and material set. The proposal is the smallest addition that carries that: two optional calls on MeshIOPlugin, loadScene and saveScene, taking a list of layer views (mesh, matrix, label, visible, material set and texture context), and a capability flag. Document drives them as it drives load and save. glTF can implement them later; nothing else changes. This is new framework code and needs approval before it is written.

5.2 Export Visible Layers as USD¶

A document operation beside Save Project, not a filter: saving changes the disk, not the document, by the project-persistence rules. One self-contained file, .usdz to send, .usdc or .usda to work with.

The .mlp stays MeshLab’s project. A second form, a root file referencing one USD file per layer (what a .mlp does), is possible and not proposed: it would be a second project format to keep in step.

5.3 Import as Separate Layers¶

The other half: each UsdGeomMesh, UsdGeomPoints and UsdGeomBasisCurves prim becomes a layer, its world transform the layer matrix and its prim name (or displayName) the label; cameras become rasters without images. A file written by 5.2 then comes back as the layers that wrote it. A menu entry beside Import Mesh; plain Open keeps flattening.

5.4 Other formats that would use the same interface¶

The interface in 5.1 is not USD-specific. Three formats MeshLab already reads and writes can hold several layers, and each is flattened into one mesh today (measured 2026-11-23: glTF bakes every node transform, 3MF every build item and component, E57 merges every scan).

Bundler, NVM, COLMAP and Agisoft camera files are photo alignments rather than scenes: an import of rasters onto existing layers.

7. Validation¶

  • Fixtures written by us, small .usda files committed under tests/data/usd/: a quad and two triangles with faceVarying coordinates and two materials by subsets; a leftHanded mesh on a Y-up stage with no unit and an invisible prim; a referenced mesh, once translated and once as a mirrored instance. Ours, so no licence question; public assets only in the local dataset, by test_data.md.

  • Round trips in tests/test_document.cpp, beside the glTF ones: save a layer, load it back, compare positions, coordinates, slot indices and the four material channels.

  • OpenUSD as a checker: when usdchecker (pip usd-core) is on the path, every file the tests write must pass it; skipped otherwise, as the datasets are.

  • By hand, once per stage: the .usdz opens in Quick Look and in Blender.

8. Cost¶

io_gltf is about 2,000 lines; io_usd is expected to be similar, plus about 150 for the scene interface of stage 2 and the two document operations. Stage 3 is a change across five places whose size its mapping table will tell.

9. Open questions¶

  1. Up axis. A rotation on the layer matrix (proposed), or baked into the coordinates as glTF’s node transforms are? Stage 1 bakes it (4.4): the layer matrix needs the load request of Load Options. And should glTF, Y-up by definition, be rotated too?

  2. Unit fallback. A stage that does not author metersPerUnit is in centimeters by the specification. Report centimeters (by definition, as glTF’s meters), or Unknown (as for a format that does not say)? Stage 0 showed the importer can tell the two apart (metersPerUnit_set); Tydra’s own fallback, 1.0, is used by neither choice. Stage 1 reports centimeters.

  3. Scene interface (5.1): approve the two optional calls, or another shape?

  4. Stage 3 timing: right after stage 1, or after stage 2?
